# Design Brief — The Drawdown Protocol

## Elevator Pitch (30 seconds)

You lead a pioneering city with a world-scale mission: prove that carbon neutrality is reachable before the climate clock runs out. The run is fifteen decisions — one every five years, 2030 to 2100 — against an adversary that never sleeps: the rest of the world's industrial blocs keep emitting, and the clock climbs toward +2.0 °C on its own. Each turn the world deals three events — droughts, fires, floods, unrest, the odd golden opportunity — and a market of four fundable policies: transform your sectors, restore sinks, keep your people on side, or spend Influence to bend other blocs' curves, because the cheapest ton anywhere is often a transition funded abroad. Summit targets are announced years in advance; combos chain; three-turn projects pay permanent powers. Reach net zero at **any** turn and you win. Hit +2.0 °C — or let happiness hit zero and the city revolt — and the run is lost; but the Knowledge you gained persists, and the next timeline starts smarter.

## Player Fantasy

"We are the ones who showed the world it was possible." The player is a **city and its people**, not an abstract force: a credible small actor with limited money and outsized conviction. They cannot command the world's blocs — they convince them, fund them, and outpace them. The fantasy has three beats:

- **Persuasion over power:** treaties and funded transitions abroad, earned with Influence; every ally slows the world's drift.
- **Stewardship of a budget:** every unit of money spent where it moves the needle most — at home or on another bloc's coal plant.
- **Turning crisis into opportunity:** a heat wave becomes a response plan; a flooded highway becomes a rail line; a social crisis opens the window for a fairer policy.

At first boot the player also chooses **who their city is** — a trade-rich Port City, a wealthy-but-filthy Industrial City, or (once unlocked) a Political Capital — and that identity tilts the whole run.

## Player Goals

- **In a run:** drive global net emissions (yours plus the world's blocs, minus absorption) to zero at any turn before warming reaches +2.0 °C — funding the market's best offers, answering the turn's crises, hitting the announced summit targets, and keeping happiness above the revolt line the whole way.
- **Across runs:** earn Knowledge Points and unlock the Knowledge tree (affordable EVs, healthy sobriety, a new city archetype...) so future runs start with real insight; defeats teach too — losing to a revolt permanently unlocks the Public Support Fund.
- **Emotionally:** move from "the whole world is against us" to "the whole world is with us" — hope earned through cooperation and competence.

## Win / Fail States

| State | Condition | Player reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Win** | Net emissions ≤ 0 at **any turn** before the tipping point | "The drawdown moment. The world absorbs more than it emits — years before the deadline." |
| **Loss A (tipping point)** | Warming reaches **+2.0 °C** at any point — the clock hits 100% | "The limit for a safe human world is breached." (Paris Agreement ceiling; see Concept.md sources) |
| **Loss B (revolt)** | Happiness reaches **0** at any point | "We governed past the people, and the city rose." |
| **Loss C (timeout)** | Year 2100 reached with net emissions still > 0 | "We survived the century, but the job is not done." |
| **Warning** | Warming crosses **+1.5 °C** → **Overshoot** state | Events intensify; sinks degrade; the pressure is on. Not a loss — a klaxon. |
| **Always** | Run end grants Knowledge Points (never less than 1; more for wins) | "Even a failed timeline teaches us something permanent." |

Every terminal state is **one readable gauge**: the climate clock (a single dial from +1.0 °C at 0% to +2.0 °C at 100%, starting at 30%), the happiness meter, or the calendar. The clock is the explicit adversary — it climbs every turn because the world's blocs keep emitting, and the HUD shows its next-turn forecast so the player always knows how long they have.

## Target Audience

- **Primary:** players of strategic management and rogue-lite games (Frostpunk, Terra Nil, Slay the Spire, Balatro) who enjoy readable systems, build variety, and meaningful trade-offs. PC, mouse-first, runs of 10–20 minutes.
- **Secondary:** climate-curious players and educators drawn to a science-grounded, cooperative, non-doomer take (Paris Agreement thresholds, IPCC sufficiency, Project Drawdown solutions, EAT-Lancet diet shift) — every card carries a codex entry naming its real-world solution.
- **Not targeting:** hardcore grand-strategy players expecting deep per-country economic simulation, or players seeking pure sandbox creativity.

## Session Shape

- One run: **15 turns of five years each** (2030–2100), 10–20 minutes; a turn is a substantial tactical puzzle — three events, a market of four offers, up to five cards funded, maybe a combo, a summit deadline looming — resolved in seconds once chosen.
- The first two turns must teach the loop by playing it: read the clock and the crisis bar, fund a matching offer, watch the crisis contained and rewarded, feel the pillars move.
- Run end always flows through the **post-mortem** — a named pivotal turn ("the revolt began on turn 8") — into the Knowledge tree: spend Knowledge Points, optionally switch city archetype, start a new timeline on a freshly generated world.

## One-Line Player Promise

*Every five years, three crises and four offers; every treaty, a bent world curve; every defeat, knowledge the world keeps.*
