# Risk Register — The Drawdown Protocol

Top production and design risks, with mitigations. Review at every milestone; add new
entries rather than deleting resolved ones (mark them Done).

| # | Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Diplomacy scope creep** — the "deal with other countries" fantasy balloons into AI opinion simulation and negotiation UI | Design/Prod | High | High | World actors stay pure data curves with two card levers (Fund a Transition, Emissions Treaty) inside the existing card system; anything richer waits until the loop is proven fun (golden rules 3, 11) |
| 2 | **Balance feels random, not strategic** — players cannot reconstruct why warming or happiness moved | Design | Medium | High | All formulas visible in `../Phase_1/01_Balance_Model.md`; per-change log lines; the clock forecast; deterministic seeds; tune via data files only |
| 3 | **Dominant strategy: diplomacy spam** — DIP2/DIP4/DIP5 touch the whole ledger and may outclass home cards once allies exist | Design | Medium | Medium | Actor cuts bottom out at bloc floors; DIP4/DIP5 carry raised market weights but still must be dealt; playtest metric: diplomacy pick-rate; balance levers ready (Influence cost, floors, trend cuts) |
| 4 | **Happiness death spiral frustrates** — low H cuts income, causing crises, cutting H further, ending in a revolt | Design | Medium | High | Spiral is intentional but must stay escapable: Wellbeing Fund, social-crisis policy window, Universal Services project, Knowledge nodes; the revolt post-mortem names the causes and losing this way permanently unlocks the Public Support Fund; watch failure interviews in playtests |
| 5 | **Late-game money hoarding** — winners bank surplus with nothing to buy | Design | Medium | Low | Much reduced from the yearly model (anchors now end ~1 000, not ~6 500): bigger card costs, project upkeep, summit stakes and actor funding all drain the treasury; the post-mortem calls out passing with ≥ 150 banked; keep watching |
| 6 | **Procgen world map rabbit hole** — building tile-level world generation before the loop is fun | Prod | Medium | High | MVP procgen is parameter-level only (starting values per seed); isometric map is a nice-to-have that follows fun, never precedes it |
| 7 | **Preachy tone undermines the message** — lecture text instead of systems | Design | Medium | Medium | Pillar 5 reject list; all climate arguments must be expressed as mechanics; codex entries state real-world solutions factually; flavor text reviewed for moralizing |
| 8 | **Event RNG feels punitive** — a bad stretch of draws reads as unfair | Design | Medium | Medium | Every event carries an opportunity rider (pillar 2); damage scaled by player-built Resilience; probabilities published in the event table |
| 9 | **Art cost of the grey-to-solarpunk arc** — the visual payoff is expensive for a small team | Prod | High | Medium | Ship MVP with abstract dashboard + palette shifts (CanvasModulate-style tinting); full tile art only after the loop is validated |
| 10 | **Science drift** — tuned numbers quietly contradict cited sources | Design | Medium | Medium | Every deliberate simplification is written in `../Phase_1/06_Assumptions.md` so it can be challenged; concept doc lists sources per mechanic; validator enforces a codex entry per card |
| 11 | **Timeline slip** — 6-week plan compresses under solo/small-team reality | Prod | High | Medium | Phase gates from Plan.md; cut from nice-to-have list first; vertical slice by end of week 2 (golden rule 14) |
| 12 | **Combo-chain inflation** — chained combo rewards plus crisis-response payouts turn the mid-game economy into a money printer | Design | High | High | Reward multiplier capped at ×2; Knowledge combo rewards pay first-fire-only; response rewards priced below avoided damage; rate-corridor watch in `../Phase_1/05` |
| 13 | **Answer-everything dominance** — if answering every crisis is always affordable, the crisis draw stops being a decision | Design | Medium | High | The market fixed most of this: answers must be *dealt* (only the cheapest one is guaranteed), and answering competes with actor funding and summit prep for the same turn; keep watching answer rates in the batch |
| 14 | **Project passives stack too hard** — completed-project passives (income, absorption/turn) compound for the rest of the run | Design | Medium | Medium | One attempt per project per run, max 2 active, three turns of real upkeep as the price; passives are top-tier tuning levers in `data/projects.json` |
| 15 | **Turn overload** — 3 events + 4 offers + combos + projects + a summit deadline in one turn overwhelms new players | Design/UX | Medium | High | The market bounds the choice space to 4–5 visible offers; crisis panel states threat and answer tags plainly; tutorial teaches one concept per step; response cards are cheap, forgiving first answers |
| 16 | **Market RNG frustration** — the deal never shows the card the player's plan needs | Design | Medium | Medium | Deliberate roguelike tension, bounded: the guarantee rule always deals at least one crisis-answering offer; archetype leans make each city's market predictable in flavor; DIP4/DIP5 carry raised weights so the global levers stay reachable |
| 17 | **World-actor despair** — the blocs' automatic drift reads as unwinnable rather than urgent | Design | Medium | High | Drift is visible and forecastable on the clock; every ally visibly damps it; one Fund a Transition (−6 Gt) is the single biggest one-card swing in the game — teach it early; floors guarantee diplomacy is never wasted |
| 18 | **Risk-card feel-bad** — a failed Fusion Moonshot at the wrong moment reads as the game cheating | Design | Low | Medium | Odds printed on the card; only two risk cards exist; failure costs are bounded (−4 happiness / nothing); resolved on a dedicated RNG stream so replays are honest |
| 19 | **Summit whiplash** — a missed target's penalty compounds a run already going badly | Design | Medium | Medium | Targets announced turns in advance (HUD line + banner); penalties cost Influence/Happiness, never the clock itself; the post-mortem weights missed summits so the lesson is named |
